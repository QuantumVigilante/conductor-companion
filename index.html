Problem, user and assumptions

The conductor has one free hand, and the bus keeps moving.

The brief asks for a digital version of the thermal-printer ticketing machine. A conductor keeps selling through a jerky ride, often while collecting cash. Looking down for more than a glance means missing a passenger or losing balance.

I treat the job as three tasks done in order: choose a stop, count passengers, issue. The design removes every step that is not one of those, and puts what remains where one thumb lands without moving the grip.

What has been reported

In 2025 the conductors' union said the machines sometimes skip ticket serial numbers. Inspectors then see more tickets than passengers and send conductors notices, so the conductor is blamed for a printing fault. ETV Bharat

Free travel for women is moving from a paper pink ticket to a card tapped on the conductor's machine. Reports say it is unclear what happens when a card does not scan. MediaNama

A 2022 Daily Pioneer report is headlined with passengers asking conductors to come to them for tickets. Daily Pioneer

In Indore, a different city, commuters said rush hour brought wrong fares and arguments over change when they had no exact amount. That is why the change screen exists. Free Press Journal

What this changed in the design

  • Speed first. On a packed bus the conductor cannot reach everyone, so a sale has to take seconds. A group is issued by repeat taps on one row, and Repeat last covers regular groups.
  • An unbroken ticket log. Every ticket is numbered and stored in order, and every undo is recorded. A mismatch can then be traced to a cause instead of landing on the conductor. The end-of-trip summary is built on this.
  • Free travel needs a fallback. The card tap is the main path. If the card does not scan, the conductor can still issue a free ticket with one extra tap, so the passenger is never refused.
  • Tickets from where the conductor stands. The screen is made for one hand in the aisle, so the conductor can sell without going back to a seat.

Reachability strategy

Everything that is tapped sits where the thumb already is.

The thumb pivots from the bottom corner of the grip. The lower part of the screen is easy, the middle is a stretch, and the top is out of reach without shifting the grip. So the top only shows information, and every control lives below it.

Key decisions with rationale

Each choice answers a physical problem on the bus.

Tap the stop again to add a passenger

The target is already under the thumb. A stepper or keypad adds a second target to find while the bus sways.

Cost: a mis-tap adds a passenger. Touch and hold on the row removes one.

One wide Issue button at the bottom

It is the most pressed control, so it is the largest and nearest the thumb. Its label carries the total, so the amount is checked before it is pressed.

Cost: it takes space from the list. Five stops fit, and the list is ordered by likelihood.

Actions fire on touch-up, with a short debounce

A jerk drops the finger on the wrong place. Firing on release lets the conductor slide off a wrong target to cancel. A short debounce ignores double taps caused by the bounce.

Cost: a slightly later response. It is small enough to stay inside the 4 second budget.

A light, high-contrast theme

Navy on white, in heavy weights, with no thin lines or pale greys. Small text is never below 12 px, and numbers are large. Sunlight washes out low-contrast screens first.

Cost: a bright screen at night. A night theme is a next step.

Hindi and English on every stop

Both names show on each row, with Hindi in Devanagari. A toggle picks which one leads, and it never adds a step to a sale.

Cost: a taller row. It is worth it so passengers can also read the stop.

Feedback without looking

Three haptic patterns tell success, error and undo apart in the hand. A short optional tone backs them up in noisy traffic.

Where the 4 seconds go

Three passengers to one stop is four taps. I assumed about 0.6 s per tap on a moving bus. That is an estimate to be measured, not a result.

What I would test next with real conductors

Five checks, each with a pass mark.

Time to issue

Five conductors, ten three-passenger sales each, on a moving bus.

Pass mark: at least 9 of 10 sales under 4 s.

Reach map

Mark where each thumb lands with the phone held as they normally do. Redraw the zones from that.

Pass mark: Issue and the nearest three stops inside the easy zone for every conductor.

Glare

Read the glance layer and fare column outdoors at noon, at arm's length.

Pass mark: every number read correctly on the first look.

Mis-tap and undo

Watch for wrong counts and wrong stops. See whether touch and hold and the undo bar are found without help.

Pass mark: errors corrected in under 3 s, with no explanation.

Left hand and Hindi

Run the mirror layout and the Hindi-first setting with the conductors who use them.

Pass mark: the same sale times as the default layout.
